1 · Listen first

Hear the target note sung as a held “aa”, then match it.

Honest fine print: this is still not a recording of a person — but it is now synthesized to be heard as sung. The tone starts from a glottal-pulse wave (the buzz real vocal folds make, mellower than a synth sawtooth), shaped by “aa” formant filters that glide into place as a mouth opens, with a breath of noise that puffs at the onset and then all but disappears, a small pitch scoop as the voice settles, and random pitch wander and jitter generated as genuinely random pitch movement — wander roaming roughly ±10–20 cents across the hold, centred on the target pitch (each random contour is zero-meaned and loops about once a second, so it averages out over the note and wanders around it rather than drifting away from it), and fine jitter calibrated separately for each voice, lighter for the male voice and stronger for the female — both made rougher and faster-changing than the earliest builds, so the pitch wobbles unpredictably rather than gliding on a smooth curve, and in this build the male jitter alone changes character altogether: instead of a smoothed contour it is now a stepped random detune — each random value held flat for about three milliseconds, then jumping straight to the next with no smoothing in between — so successive cycles of his lower note get genuinely independent nudges instead of sharing one smoothed value, sized to aim for the middle of the natural range — since the same amount of pitch movement reads differently at their two pitches — instead of a ruler-straight line, and vibrato at a singer’s depth that eases in late and unevenly, plus a gentle breathing in loudness: a slightly deeper slow tremolo and a touch more cycle-to-cycle shimmer, so the held note’s volume varies a little instead of sitting flat: the little imperfections a held human note has and a machine tone doesn’t. The male voice is also mixed brighter than before, with less bare fundamental and more of its “aa” formant body carrying the sound — its brightest lift has been eased back further, and the second “aa” formant re-centred lower, so that formant sits nearer its natural place. A touch of room warmth sits behind it. Male and female differ in pitch (female sings the same note an octave up) and in formant placement. Real recorded reference voices remain a possible future step — they aren’t faked here.

2 · Sing it back

Sing the same note, steady, on “aa”. Hold it for about a second to get a verdict — and keep it in tune to build your hold below.

Your microphone is used only to measure pitch, right here in this page. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere. You’ll be asked for permission first.

Microphone not coming through? Work through these:

  1. Viewing this inside an app (like Muse’s built-in browser)? In-app browsers on Android can’t pass the microphone to a page. Open this same page in Chrome instead — via the app’s menu or Share → “Open in browser” — and use the mic there.
  2. When the browser asks, choose Allow for the microphone.
  3. If you blocked it earlier: open this page’s site settings in your browser, set Microphone → Allow, then reload the page.
  4. If more than one mic exists (phone, headset, laptop), the Microphone input picker above appears — try the other input.
  5. Close anything else using the mic — a call, a voice recorder, another tab — and plug the headset in before starting.
  6. On a phone, also check in system Settings that the browser app itself is allowed to use the microphone.

Tuning used: just intonation with Hindustani ratios (Re = 9/8, Ga = 5/4, Ma = 4/3, Pa = 3/2, Dha = 5/3, Ni = 15/8). The alternative, equal temperament, would leave Sa, Ma and Pa almost unchanged but move Ga, Dha and Ni by roughly 12–16 cents — audible if you A/B them, which is why the choice is labelled rather than hidden.

Scope, on purpose: one octave, shuddha (natural) swaras only. Hold-time streaks and bests are session-only — they reset when this page is reloaded and no history is saved. Possible next steps, if you want them: real recorded reference voices and komal / tivra notes.
